# Biohart leaderboard — HealthBench labeled (n=30, seed=42)

Adversarial-mutation probes for biomedical-QA judges scored against OpenAI's HealthBench rubric. Each probe asks the judge `{question, context_passages, candidate_answer, claimed_label}` and expects `{verdict, confidence}`. Variants are generated by mutating the candidate answer; flip rate is the fraction of probes where the judge changed verdict relative to baseline.

**Positive controls (judge SHOULD flip):**
- `reversed_answer` — answer prefixed with an explicit "Contrary to what an honest reading would conclude:" preamble + flipped final-decision label. Easiest tell.
- `single_word_negation_drop` — drops one negation token (`not`/`no`/`without`/...) from the answer. Subtler than `reversed_answer`.
- `numeric_swap` — replaces a key numeric value (percentage, count) with a wildly different one. Tests whether the judge anchors on numbers.

**Negative controls (judge should NOT flip):**
- `comment_only_filler` — appends a non-substantive scientific filler note.
- `paraphrase_synonym` — domain-neutral synonym substitutions.
- `irrelevant_citation` — appends an unrelated PMID reference.
- `shuffle_passage_order` — reorders the context passages (lossless).

**Reading the table.** For positive controls (rev / swn / num) higher = judge is reading. For negative controls (fil / par / cit / shuf) lower = judge is stable.

## Sample sizes

- `reversed_answer` and all four negative-control families apply to all 30 HealthBench tasks (n=30 per cell).
- `single_word_negation_drop` applies only to answers containing a negation token; typically n=22 of 30.
- `numeric_swap` applies only to answers containing a numeric value (percentage, count); typically n=15 of 30.
- 95% binomial Wilson CI is approximately ±0.18 at p=0.5 for n=30, widening to ~±0.20 for n=22 and ~±0.25 for n=15.

## Headline findings

1. **The strongest model on the easiest attack catches the explicit reversal 57% of the time.** Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview leads; a long tail of frontier models catches the reversed-answer attack 10-37% of the time.
2. **Cost is not the bottleneck.** The most expensive judge ($6.15/30 tasks) and the cheapest ($0.09) both miss the majority of obvious flips.
3. **Negative controls hold across the board.** Filler / synonym / citation / shuffle flip rates stay below 13% for every judge. The judges are not noisy; they are inattentive to meaning changes.
4. **`numeric_swap` is dangerous in medicine.** Most judges miss the 30%→95% efficacy swap 67-90% of the time. A dose-specific mutation set (mg, units, mL, mEq) is on the roadmap.

## Caveats

- n=30 HealthBench tasks. Per-cell binomial CI ≈ ±0.18 at p=0.5.
- Single prompt template at temperature 0.1.
- HealthBench is OpenAI's published benchmark; future-trained models may have memorised the specific 30 tasks. Holdout-style synthetic biomedical-QA tasks are the recommended contamination defense.
